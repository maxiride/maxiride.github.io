About me

I studied mechatronics, then followed the software side into information systems engineering — though I’ve been taking things apart since before either degree existed for me. These days that shows up at home: Home Assistant runs the house, and the alarm system watching over it is one I built from scratch, panel to sensor, on a professional Dahua stack.

For years I worked as a freelancer chasing state-of-the-art under the hood, assuming that doing things right would be reward enough. It taught me otherwise: the market pays for what works, not for what’s elegant underneath. I learned to find the trade-off instead — once rewriting a CRM I’d built for a ten-person company because I’d normalized the database into joins nobody needed. That doesn’t mean quality slides. The shortcuts I take are deliberate, and I leave a note when I take one.

I don’t like guessing, especially under pressure. When a client’s servers kept freezing, the vendor’s fix was a quote for new hardware — eighty thousand euros, on machines that were simply old. I pushed to find the actual cause instead, traced it to an undocumented VMware bug, and the servers have run clean since. I’d rather spend the time understanding a problem than pay to make it disappear — and I want the systems I build to explain themselves the same way: clear logs, documented decisions, APIs that mean what they say.

I mentor junior engineers when I can. I still recognize the itch to do everything by the book, and I like showing that the book is a starting point, not the destination. Outside of work, if there’s water nearby, I’m happier — the closest thing I have to a long-term plan involves a sailboat.
